The Guardrail That Timed Out Open

2026-07-28 · dispatch

The trust and safety lead flagged it on Monday, and she flagged it because the numbers were too good. Saturday night was the companion app's busiest window and usually its worst for policy violations. This Saturday, output moderation had caught zero violations between 20:00 and 22:00. Not fewer. Zero.

Then a user's screenshot reached the support inbox. The app's character had produced a reply that should never have passed moderation, timestamped 21:14 on Saturday.

The on-call engineer pulled the moderation logs for that window. There were far fewer log lines than there should have been. About a third of the responses in those two hours had no moderation result at all.

Every response from the model went through an output moderation call to a third-party classifier before reaching the user. The integration had a timeout of 800 milliseconds, set by the team that owned the chat latency SLO. On timeout, the code logged a debug line and sent the response anyway. The comment above that branch said # don't block chat on moderation hiccups.

⚠ Moderation had an 800 ms timeout, and on timeout the response went out unchecked. Vendor p95 rose to 1.2 s. Violations “dropped” because a third of responses were never checked.

On Saturday evening, the moderation vendor had its own traffic spike. Its p95 latency rose from 300 milliseconds to 1.2 seconds for about two hours. Roughly 34% of moderation calls timed out. Each of those responses went straight to the user, unchecked. About 58,000 of them.

The chat latency dashboard stayed green the whole time, because the timeout protected it. The moderation dashboard showed a beautiful night, because it only counted violations in responses it had actually checked. Nothing anywhere counted the checks that never happened.

“Nothing anywhere counted the checks that never happened.”

The team had made a trade-off without writing it down. Latency was protected by an SLO, an alert and a dashboard. Safety coverage had none of those. When the two collided, the code chose latency, every time, silently.

The fixes put that trade-off back in plain view. A moderation timeout now fails closed: the user sees a short “one moment” message, and the system retries moderation once before sending or replacing the response. The timeout is owned jointly by the latency and safety teams. Moderation coverage, the share of responses that received a verdict, is now a metric with its own SLO of 99.9%, and it pages when it drops. And the moderation vendor's latency is on the main dashboard, because a dependency that can switch off a safety control needs to be watched like one.

Re-running the 58,000 unchecked responses through moderation found 212 violations, most of them minor. The trust and safety lead's instinct, that zero was the wrong number, was the only detector that worked.

A safety check that times out open is a safety check with a latency SLO and no safety SLO.
