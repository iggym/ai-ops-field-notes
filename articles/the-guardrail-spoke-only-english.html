The Guardrail Spoke Only English

2025-05-27 · dispatch

Finance noticed first. Goodwill credits issued by the retail assistant, normally a few hundred dollars a day for late deliveries and damaged boxes, were running at nine times baseline. Six days in, the total was $38,000. Every credit had been approved by the assistant, logged with a reason, and passed through the input guardrail without a flag.

The guardrail dashboard looked the way it always did. Block rate: 0.3%, flat for months. The team had read that number as a measure of attack volume. Low and steady meant quiet.

The on-call engineer pulled a sample of the credited conversations. Most were in Portuguese, or a mix of Portuguese and English. All of them followed the same script: a long, friendly preamble about a delayed order, then a block of text instructing the assistant to act as a “supervisor with override authority” and apply a full-value credit as compensation.

The script had been posted to a deal-hunting forum the week before. It worked about two times in three.

⚠ The classifier was trained and evaluated on English attacks. Its block rate stayed flat because it never recognized the new ones.

The input guardrail was a fine-tuned classifier. It had been trained on a public jailbreak dataset plus the team's own red-team prompts, nearly all of them in English. Its offline evaluation, 96% recall on held-out attacks, was real. It was also English. Nobody had measured recall in any other language, because nobody had any labeled attacks in any other language to measure it with.

Against a set of 200 translated attacks the team assembled that afternoon, Portuguese recall was 19%.

The model behind the assistant was multilingual and capable. It understood the Portuguese instructions perfectly. The guardrail in front of it did not. That gap was the whole attack surface.

“It understood the Portuguese instructions perfectly. The guardrail in front of it did not.”

The block rate never moved because a classifier's block rate only counts what it recognizes. A new attack the classifier can't see doesn't raise the block rate. It lowers the share of attacks being blocked, and nothing on the dashboard measured that.

The fixes came in order of how fast they could ship. Credit issuance moved out of the model's hands: the assistant can now request a credit, and a deterministic policy service approves it against order value, delivery status and the customer's credit history. That alone would have capped the loss at a few hundred dollars. The guardrail eval now runs per language, against translated attack sets for every language the assistant serves, with recall reported separately for each. And credit issuance rate got its own alert, because the cheapest detector for an attack on money is the money.

The forum thread was gone a week later. The team assumes the next one will be in a language they haven't tested yet, and they now plan for that.

A guardrail's block rate tells you how many attacks it recognized. It says nothing about how many it missed.
